A free Mac app for parents

Explicit content on your Mac, covered the moment it appears.

SentryScreen is a small app for your family's Mac. It checks what is on the screen, on the Mac itself, and drops a solid cover over explicit content in any app or browser. Free and open source.

Download for Mac Source on GitHub

Free · open source (AGPL-3.0) · for Mac

  • Nothing from your screen leaves the Mac

    The screen is checked on the Mac, in memory. The app never keeps, uploads, or sends screen images. (A developer command-line option you run yourself can save crops; see Privacy.)

  • Your child cannot remove it or change its settings

    Settings need your PIN and removal needs the administrator password. Protection starts again at every login. The one way around it is under Honest limits.

  • Free, and the code is open

    No account, no subscription, no trial. Every line of the app and the detector it runs is published under AGPL-3.0. Read the source

What it does

The whole screen, not just the browser

The cover is a plain solid rectangle. Nothing shows through it, it moves with the window underneath, and it says nothing more than “Content blocked”.

  • In a browser

    A page scrolls, a picture comes into view, and the cover lands on it. It stays on the picture as the page keeps scrolling.

  • It follows the window

    Drag the window across the desk and the cover rides along. Nothing shows through at any point.

  • In a chat, or any other app

    It is not a list of blocked websites. A picture sent in a chat is covered the same way, in an app we have never heard of.

  • The whole window, if it has to

    If a cover stays up for a while, the whole window is covered until the content is gone or the window is closed.

Setup

Three things you do, once

  1. Install it

    Download the newest release from GitHub and run the installer from an administrator account on the Mac.

  2. Set a parent PIN

    The PIN keeps settings with you. It is the first thing setup asks for.

  3. Grant permission

    macOS makes you switch on Screen Recording by hand, in System Settings. No app can do it for you. Setup shows you exactly where the switch is. Protection starts as soon as it is on.

Why we built it

For parents who are done worrying

SentryScreen was built by a parent of eight who was tired of the usual routine:

  • the router settings and the DNS blockers,
  • the maze of Apple's parent and child restrictions,
  • the checking and worrying that never ended, all so the kids could use a computer.

What we believe

  • No child can be shielded from explicit content every second of every day.
  • Exposure can be cut sharply, without a parent standing behind the chair.
  • One detector on the Mac itself, covering whatever appears on the display, is how. Let the Mac keep watch.

Honest limits

What it does not do

Better said before you install it than after.

  • It reacts, it does not prevent

    Content can be on screen for a fraction of a second before the cover lands, and longer if it slides in from the edge of a window.

  • It can miss things, and it can cover harmless things

    It is a detector, not a person. It can cover something harmless, and it can miss something. One layer of protection, not the whole answer.

  • Explicit means nudity and sexual images

    It does not look for violence or words.

  • It covers what is on the screen, and that is all

    It does not block websites, filter downloads, restrict apps, or read messages. Video from protected streaming services such as Netflix is hidden from every app by macOS, so it is not covered.

  • A Standard account can switch off its screen permission

    macOS lets any account reset the Screen Recording permission with a Terminal command and no password. SentryScreen then stops covering on that Mac until you turn the permission back on in System Settings › Privacy & Security.

  • It protects one screen

    If a second screen is connected to the Mac, anything shown on it is not covered. The menu names the screen that is protected.

  • Going fullscreen shows the window for a moment

    When a window enters or leaves fullscreen, macOS draws its animation above everything, our cover included. For about half a second the window shows, then it is covered again on its own.

Questions

Before you install

Can my child turn it off?

Not without your PIN or your password, with one gap. Settings need your PIN. Removing it, or clearing the covers for a few minutes, needs the Mac’s administrator password, which should be yours alone. One gap: on a Standard account, a child who knows the Terminal command can switch off SentryScreen’s Screen Recording permission without a password, and SentryScreen stops covering until you turn the permission back on.

What permission does it need, and why?

One: Screen Recording, which is how macOS lets an app see what is on the screen. You grant it by hand in System Settings › Privacy & Security during setup. Nothing is recorded. The name is Apple's, not a description of what SentryScreen does.

Does it send anything over the internet?

Only one thing, and only if you choose to: a diagnostic report. You see what is in it, and nothing is sent until you approve it on that Mac. It is never sent automatically and never contains screen content. The privacy page lists what a report holds and how long it is kept.

How do updates work?

The app does not update itself or check for updates. The Download button on this site always gets the newest version; open it and the installer replaces the old one. Notes on what changed are on the releases page.

How many Macs can I use it on?

As many as you like, with as many child accounts on each as you need. Your child’s account must be a Standard account, not an administrator one: an administrator can remove SentryScreen or clear its covers.

Can a school, library or business use it?

Yes. The license is AGPL-3.0: you can use, change and share it. If you share a changed version, or offer it to others over a network, you share your changes under the same license.

Something is wrong. Where do I ask?

Open an issue on GitHub, or see the Support page.
